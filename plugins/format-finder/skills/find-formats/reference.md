# Why the method works this way

The rules the skill follows, each with the reason for it. The numbers are rules of thumb, not audited studies, which is why they live in editable settings.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Build searches from the audience's own words and the niche's conventions | People search and caption in their own words, not in the app's marketing terms |
| Search adjacent and unrelated niches, not only the obvious keyword | The strongest structures often come from another niche and carry over |
| Find competitor apps in the app store, look at their accounts | Their accounts show what already works for the same audience |
| Recent videos only (last month or two) | Older hits say less about what works now |
| Judge a video against its own account's normal views, not raw views | Raw views mostly measure account size; the multiple measures the video |
| Small accounts with big views are the strongest signal | A small account has no audience to carry an ordinary video |
| 5-10x the account's usual views as an outlier signal | Below that, a video is within an account's normal ups and downs |
| Absolute view counts only as a rough floor (5,000 views by default), never as the main rule | A high multiple on a tiny account proves little, but raw views alone favour big accounts |
| Top ~10% of a batch (the scan flags them `top_fraction`; step 7 looks at those first) | The clearest outliers are the best place to start watching |
| Views far out of line with likes and comments are suspicious; bought reach is not evidence | Paid or boosted views say nothing about whether the structure works |
| A format must work on several accounts, not one creator | One creator's hit can come from that person, not from the format |
| Formats fade as new versions do worse; they tend to last a few months | A format whose newer videos do worse is on its way out |
| Riding a moment works; a real person's voice or likeness is a rights risk | A moment can be reused while it lasts; someone else's face or voice cannot |
| Describe structure separately from angle and hook | The structure is what carries over; the angle and hook change per app |
| Swap the app into the structure; insider humour carries over poorly | A joke that needs the original niche's context falls flat elsewhere |
| Test a few distinct formats, not variants of one | Distinct formats tell you more than small changes to one |

What no rule settles (so the tool leaves it to settings or to your judgment): how many posts make a fair normal, the exact outlier cutoff, the exact view floor, how to spot an account that had already taken off before a video (the tool's recent normal: the posts made just before it), how many accounts make a format repeatable, how to measure burnout and over how many days, the in-niche vs borrowed split, and how to tell a real format from one video reposted across a network of accounts (check-formats lists each set of accounts whose captions share a run of words, with those words; you still decide, set by set, whether they are one producer).
